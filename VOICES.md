# Voices

This book has two authors and they do not blend. A reader must be able to tell,
without guessing, whether a sentence was thought by a person or generated by a
machine.

Justichuu is the author. AI is a labeled contributor. Neither one speaks for
the other, and neither one edits inside the other's text.

## The two voices

`**Voice: Justichuu.**` opens a passage written by Justichuu. His words, his
judgment, his mistakes.

`**Voice: AI.**` opens a passage generated by an AI assistant and released only
after Justichuu chose to keep it. Labeled machine text is still machine text.
It is not testimony, not consent, and not evidence that a claim is true.

## Where the rule applies

A chapter opts in by placing this exact line directly under its heading:

```text
**Voices: separated.**
```

Inside such a chapter every paragraph sits under a voice marker. There is no
neutral prose, no shared paragraph, and no unattributed sentence. Headings are
structure and carry no voice, so a new marker must appear after each heading and
before any prose.

Chapters written before this rule existed keep the older inline attribution
style used in Comedy Gold, where each quotation names its speaker and the date
it was selected.

## Reserved slots

A slot waiting on Justichuu contains exactly this line and nothing else:

```text
_Unwritten. Justichuu writes here._
```

A reserved slot may also hold single-line HTML comments holding a question for
him. It may hold no prose. The point of the slot is that the machine cannot
fill it, cannot rough it out, and cannot leave a draft for him to lightly edit
into his own name. When he writes, he deletes the placeholder line and the
question with it.

An unwritten chapter is honest. A ghostwritten one is not.

## What AI may never do here

- Write inside a `**Voice: Justichuu.**` block, including grammar and spelling
  fixes. Corrections to his text are proposed in review, never applied in place.
- Imitate his voice, his humor, or his memories anywhere in the book.
- Invent a quotation, a date, a credential, a number, or a selection he did not
  make.
- Present its own agreement as confirmation of his idea.
- Move text from a labeled AI passage into an unlabeled one.

## What the validator checks

`python validate.py` fails the public tree when a chapter that contains
`**Voices: separated.**` does not put that line first, when a Markdown fence is
left open, when a strict chapter contains prose with no voice marker, when a
voice block is empty, when a reserved slot holds anything other than its
placeholder and questions, or when an AI block uses the human placeholder.

The validator also reports a voice ledger:

```text
python validate.py --voices
```

The ledger prints the word count for each voice and the number of reserved
slots still waiting on Justichuu. It is a measurement, not a target. A page
where the machine wrote most of the words is not automatically wrong, but the
reader gets to see it.

## The honest limit of this rule

Tooling can prove that a label is present and that a block is not mixed. It
cannot prove who moved the keys. That part is the author's word, kept in public
where it can be checked against the history of the repository.
